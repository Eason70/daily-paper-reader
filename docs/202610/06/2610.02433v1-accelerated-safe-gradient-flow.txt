Accelerated Safe Gradient Flow
Xuting Gao, Sonia Mart´ınez
Abstract—This letter introduces an accelerated safe gradi-
ent flow (A-SGF) for strongly convex constrained optimization
problems. The A-SGF filters a nominal second-order accelerated
flow through a second-order control barrier function quadratic
program. We establish conditions for strict feasibility and local
Lipschitz continuity of the resulting vector field, prove forward
invariance of a lifted safe set, and show that its unique equilib-
rium is the constrained minimizer. A variational Lyapunov anal-
ysis isolates the effect of the safety correction and gives explicit
conditions on the barrier gains under which A-SGF retains the
same convergence rate as the unconstrained Nesterov accelerated
method. A nonlinear 2D example demonstrate anytime feasibility
and faster convergence of the algorithm.
I. INTRODUCTION
The safe gradient flow (SGF) [1] casts a constrained op-
timization in terms of a safety-critical control problem. By
filtering the negative gradient through control barrier functions
(CBFs), the SGF keeps every trajectory feasible while steering
it toward a constrained local minimizer. This viewpoint is
particularly appealing when the optimization dynamics are in-
terconnected with a physical system, where intermediate infea-
sible solutions may be inadmissible. Thus, the SGF framework
has been extended to safe feedback optimization [2], [3] and
applied successfully to safety-critical optimal regulation [4].
Acceleration, on the other hand, plays a central role in
first-order optimization. Momentum methods, originating with
Polyak’s heavy-ball method [5], and Nesterov’s accelerated
gradient method [6], can significantly improve convergence.
Their continuous-time interpretations clarify the acceleration
mechanism [7], including its variational and Lyapunov per-
spectives [8], [9]. For an L-smooth and µ-strongly convex
objective, Nesterov’s accelerated method for strongly con-
vex functions (NAG-SC) exhibits the characteristic 1/√κ
convergence-rate dependence, where κ = L/µ, compared with
1/κ for standard gradient flow.
Safety-critical control further motivates the adaptation of
accelerated methods with anytime safety; however, this task
is nontrivial. Projection-based inertial dynamics can preserve
feasibility, but the resulting vector field is generally non-
smooth [10], [11]. Primal-dual accelerated flows drive a
feasibility residual to zero asymptotically and therefore do
not guarantee that the primal trajectory remains feasible at
every finite time [12]. More fundamentally, when constraints
become active, the constraint-handling mechanism modifies
the nominal accelerated dynamics. Consequently, the nominal
accelerated convergence rate is not automatically retained.
Precisely, due to the properties of the SGF, overcoming these
issues by integrating the NAG-SC with SGFs seems viable.

Motivated by these questions, we develop an accelerated
safe gradient flow for constrained, strongly convex optimiza-
tion. The proposed method takes NAG-SC as the nominal
second-order flow and filters its acceleration through a second-
order CBF quadratic program. The manuscript’s contributions
are fourfold. First, we formulate A-SGF and establish condi-
tions for strict feasibility of the CBF-QP and local Lipschitz
continuity of the resulting vector field, thereby guaranteeing
well-defined dynamics. Second, we prove forward invariance
of the lifted safe set and show that A-SGF introduces no
undesired equilibria. Third, through a Euclidean variational
analysis, we characterize how the CBF correction enters the
dissipation inequality and derive checkable conditions on the
first- and second-order CBF gains under which the nominal
1/√κ convergence rate is retained. Finally, numerical exam-
ples illustrate the safety and acceleration properties of A-SGF.1

VI. CONCLUSION
This letter introduced the A-SGF for convex optimization,
which converges to the unique constrained optimizer while
preserving anytime feasibility. We established conditions for
well-posedness and derived explicit HOCBF gain conditions
under which A-SGF retains the convergence rate of the nom-
inal accelerated flow.

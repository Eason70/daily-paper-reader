Risk-Aware Input-Constrained Safe Intercept
Guidance Against Multiple Moving Defenders
Praveen Kumar Ranjan, Member, IEEE, Abhinav Sinha, Senior Member, IEEE,
Yongcan Cao, Senior Member, IEEE, Alexander Von Moll, Senior Member, IEEE,
and Isaac E. Weintraub, Senior Member, IEEE
Abstract—This paper develops a risk-aware guidance law for
an attacker to intercept a stationary target in the presence of
multiple moving defenders while respecting the control input
limits. We represent defender threats via engagement zones
(EZs) (regions of state-space from which attacker interception
is feasible) and employ the dynamic maneuvering cue (DMC)
(minimum heading correction to exit EZs) as a deterministic
measure of geometric risk. Safety is enforced through a pre-
scribed bound on the DMC to enable controlled EZ penetration
subject to a specified risk tolerance. For multiple defenders,
the corresponding safety constraints are combined through a
smooth-minimum aggregation. The proposed guidance strategy
drives the attacker toward the target when the engagement
remains safe and transitions to an avoidance maneuver as
the prescribed safety boundary is approached. The resulting
online optimization-free guidance law directly bounds the ap-
plied lateral acceleration and initiates a bounded preemptive
maneuver before corrective action is required. Closed-loop anal-
ysis establishes preservation of the prescribed risk constraints
under pointwise bounded-input feasibility and finite-time target
interception under a sustained target-accessibility condition.
Numerical studies validate the proposed strategy and illustrate
the tradeoff between allowable geometric risk and interception
performance.
Index Terms—Engagement zones, dynamic maneuvering cue,
input constraints, control barrier functions, safe guidance,
target interception.
I. INTRODUCTION
Autonomous systems are increasingly being deployed in
contested environments and must accomplish mission ob-
jectives while remaining resilient to adversarial threats [1].
In missions such as target interception, air defense, and
precision strike, guidance design must therefore account not
only for terminal accuracy but also for operational constraints
imposed by the defended environment. Target-Attacker-
Defender (TAD) games have provided a useful framework
for studying such conflicts [2]–[4]. Existing attacker-side
TAD and pursuit-evasion studies have developed strategies
using differential games, optimal guidance, robust control,

rely on assumptions about defender strategies, simplified
engagement dynamics, or tractable game structures that may
become restrictive in adversarial settings.
Alternatively, interception and obstacle-avoidance methods
often enforce safety through minimum-distance or fixed
stand-off constraints [10]–[13]. However, such distance-based
safety constraints do not fully capture the directional nature
of defender threats and can lead to conservative avoidance
behavior. Geometric approaches, such as velocity-obstacle
[14] and collision-cone methods [15], use relative motion
to identify velocity or geometric regions associated with
future collision risk. However, these formulations are pri-
marily intended for collision or kinematic conflict avoid-
ance and do not directly represent the finite interception
capability of a defender. For interception scenarios involving
active defenders, engagement zones offer a geometry-based
threat representation that characterizes when the attacker
becomes vulnerable to interception [16]–[18]. Therefore,
the EZ boundary differentiates threat levels that may arise
at the same attacker-defender separation and result in less
conservative safety constraints.
Although EZs reduce the conservatism of fixed stand-off
constraints, the resulting unsafe region can still be conser-
vative because penetrations with different levels of recov-
erability are treated equivalently. This motivates the use of
the dynamic maneuvering cue (DMC) [19], which measures
recoverability in terms of the heading correction needed
to restore a safe engagement condition. This enables the
allowable threat exposure to be specified through a prescribed
DMC tolerance, rather than by strict EZ avoidance. Extending
this risk-aware formulation to practical engagements further
requires that the prescribed safety maneuvers remain feasi-
ble under actuator constraints. Existing approaches account
for actuator limitations either indirectly through quadratic
costs or feasibility conditions [20], [21], or by explicitly
incorporating bounded acceleration or actuator saturation into
the vehicle dynamics, e.g., [21], or through an admissibil-
ity preserving input realization (APIR) [22]–[24]. However,
simultaneously enforcing DMC-based risk constraints for
multiple moving defenders under finite control authority
introduces additional challenges. Therefore, this work is
motivated by the need for a guidance strategy that simultane-

penetration, handles multiple moving defenders, and respects
the attacker’s physical maneuvering limits without relying on

The main contributions of this work are summarized as
follows:
• We develop a risk-aware intercept guidance framework
that bounds defender exposure through a prescribed
DMC tolerance to enables controlled penetration inside
the threat zone rather than strict avoidance.
• We propose an online optimization-free safety-critical
guidance law for multiple moving defenders that aggre-
gates the individual risk constraints through a smooth
minimum and combines a preemptive avoidance maneu-
ver with the target-interception maneuver, while directly
respecting the attacker’s lateral-acceleration bound.
• We establish risk-constraint and input-bound preserva-
tion under pointwise feasibility, together with finite-time
target interception once sustained target-directed motion
is permitted by the risk constraints.

V. CONCLUSIONS
This work addressed safe target interception in contested
environments by allowing the attacker to trade strict defender
avoidance for a prescribed level of recoverable geometric
exposure. The proposed formulation uses the DMC-based
risk tolerance to distinguish between unsafe penetrations
that require excessive recovery maneuvers and admissi-
ble penetrations from which the attacker can still recover.
The developed guidance law combines multiple defender
constraints, anticipates loss of effective turning authority,
while respecting the available lateral-acceleration limits. The
closed-loop results show that the prescribed risk bounds
and actuator constraints can be maintained while preserving
bounded guidance behavior and enabling target interception
under appropriate engagement conditions. Future work will
consider adaptive defender-dependent risk tolerances, uncer-
tainty in defender parameters and measurements, and three-
dimensional experimental validation.

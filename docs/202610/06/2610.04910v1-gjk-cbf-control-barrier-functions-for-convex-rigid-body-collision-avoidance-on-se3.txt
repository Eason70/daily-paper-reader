# GJK-CBF: Control Barrier Functions for Convex Rigid Body Collision Avoidance on SE(3) 

Yi-Hsuan Chen 1, Shuo Liu 2, Wei Xiao 3, Michael Otte 1, Calin Belta 4

Abstract — Collision avoidance among convex bodies is afundamental problem in robotics. Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) provide a practical framework for real-time safety filtering due to their computational efficiency. For general convex bodies, exact separation measures, such as distance or scaling factor, are typically computed through optimization. Existing CBF formulations often obtain the required gradient via differentiable optimization (diffOpt), adding computational overhead. In contrast, we leverage the Gilbert-Johnson-Keerthi (GJK) algorithm to obtain the current witness pair—the pair of points realizing the minimum distance or penetration depth— and formulate a CBF, termed GJK-CBF, whose gradient is constructed directly from the relative rigid-body motion of the witness pair, without resorting to diffOpt . This formulation applies to both 2D and 3D environments across a broad class of convex body pairs, provided that at least one in each one-to-one interaction is strictly convex. The proposed GJK-CBF is validated in various scenarios, including multi-robot position swapping in both 2D and 3D, navigation through a vertical slit in 3D, and its applicability to manipulators. The results demon-strate collision-free motion across all scenarios while reducing the conservativeness introduced by geometric approximations, particularly in narrow environments. A video of all simulations is available at https://youtu.be/eDvscHhGIj0 .

I. I NTRODUCTION 

One of the key difficulties in robotic navigation is to si-multaneously satisfy system kinematic/dynamic constraints, safety requirements, and actuation limits. The problem is further complicated when collision avoidance must explic-itly account for the geometry of both the ego robot and nearby robots/obstacles. For each pair of bodies, referred to as a collision pair, the separation distance depends on their relative configuration, i.e., position and orientation, and is nontrivial to evaluate and differentiate. The notion of configuration obstacles [1] was proposed to account for robot geometry via Minkowski operations and has been widely used in motion planning [2]–[4]. While the same ge-ometric representation underlies classical collision-detection algorithms such as Gilbert-Johnson-Keerthi (GJK) [5] and the Expanding Polytope Algorithm (EPA) [6] to characterize distance proximity, these methods are not designed for safety control synthesis.

Control Barrier Functions (CBFs) [8], on the other hand, provide a systematic approach to synthesizing safe con-trollers. CBF safety constraints can be implemented on-the-fly, either jointly with Control Lyapunov Functions (CLFs) [9] or as minimally invasive safety filters to simultaneously address safety and stabilization objectives. Several CBF formulations have been developed for collision avoidance, and we focus only on those that explicitly account for the geometries of both bodies. Optimization-based approaches solve an optimization problem to compute a measure of separation, such as a scaling factor [10], minimum distance [11], [12], smooth distance approximation [13], or exact Signed Distance (SD) [14]. This measure is then used to define a CBF, and its gradient is obtained through differ-entiable optimization (diffOpt) [15]. A similar workflow has also been applied to uncertain obstacles [16] using constrained convex generators. In contrast, optimization-free approaches construct CBFs based on polytope vertices [17], supporting hyperplanes for ellipsoids [7] and polytopes [18], or Bernstein-polynomial SD fields [19]. Such approaches arXiv:2610.04910v1 [cs.RO] 4 Oct 2026 are computationally efficient, but generally rely on surrogate distance measures. These two classes of methods appear to suggest a trade-off between geometric exactness and computational efficiency. However, although the SD sensitivity in our previous work [14] was derived through diffOpt, the resulting gradient expression was independent of the sensitivity of the optimizer but depended only on the optimizer itself, i.e., the witness pair (the pair of points realizing minimum distance when separated or penetration depth when intersecting). Motivated by this, we propose GJK-CBF 1, a CBF framework that re-tains the exact distance and obtains its gradient directly from the (instantaneous) witness pair without diffOpt (Fig. 1). Related witness-pair-based distance gradients have ap-peared in earlier studies [20]–[24]. Proximity queries like GJK have been incorporated into real-time CBF safety filters, while obtaining their gradients through diffOpt with ran-domized smoothing [25] or a Schur-complement acceleration [26]. Another related work is [7], which designs CBFs using rotating supporting hyperplanes and derives a closed-form gradient by exploiting the special algebraic structure of el-lipsoids, but does not directly generalize to other geometries. The most closely related work to our approach is [27], which formulates the distance-based CBF directly on SE (3) , but does not provide an explicit form for the distance gradient. To the best of our knowledge, existing approaches do not provide a CBF formulation with an explicit expression for the exact distance gradient for general convex rigid bodies. To address this gap, we develop GJK-CBF, in which, once a witness pair is given, the exact distance is immediately determined, and its gradient can be obtained accordingly. The main contributions of this paper are as follows:  

> •

The proposed GJK-CBF is geometry-agnostic within the class of convex rigid bodies and applies in both 2D and 3D workspaces, provided that at least one body in each collision pair is strictly convex (Def. 3).  

> •

We derive an explicit expression for the distance gra-dient on SE (3) solely from the witness pair without diffOpt, enabling optimization-based distance solvers to be replaced by GJK proximity queries. We also provide equivalent Euler-angle and quaternion representations.  

> •

We show that the proposed CBF framework can be implemented in a decentralized fashion, assuming that the information associated with each collision pair is accessible to the corresponding robots.  

> •

We validate our method across different 2D/3D scenar-ios and compare it with the supporting-hyperplane-CBF [7], showing that our method is less conservative while remaining collision-free. The rest of this paper is organized as follows. Section II provides preliminaries and Section III introduces the problem formulation. Section IV contains our main results on the proposed GJK-CBF. Section V presents simulation results and Section VI concludes the paper.

A. Comparative study 

We first compare our method with the supporting-hyperplane-CBF [7], designed for ellipsoidal robots. We consider six heterogeneous ellipsoidal robots uniformly dis-tributed on a circle of radius r0 = 10 m and indexed counter-clockwise from the purple robot on the x-axis (Fig. 2). Each robot’s goal is the initial position of its diametrically opposite robot. The robots have semi-axis lengths of (2 .52 , 1.26) ,

(2 .59 , 0.56) , (1 .82 , 1.40) , (2 .66 , 1.54) , (2 .24 , 1.19) , and 

(2 .10 , 0.42) m, respectively. All initial orientations are zero, with desired orientations of 40 , 90 , −25 , 10 , 0, −70 degrees, respectively. For both methods, we set kv,i = 1 .2, kω,i = 0 .8,and use W = diag(1 , 1, 5) for all robots. For [7], the auxiliary variable’s virtual-input weight (in the CBF-QP) and gain are 1 and kz, 0 = 10 , respectively. We simulate for 

T = 8 s with ∆t = 5 ms. As shown in Fig. 2, the proposed GJK-CBF maintains positive pairwise separation for all time, whereas, under the parameters used here, the supporting-hyperplane-CBF results in inter-robot collisions, which might arise from inter-sample effects. The baseline nevertheless has lower computation time by avoiding explicit pairwise distance queries at each control loop. The trade-off is a higher-dimensional local QP due to auxiliary states and controls for the supporting hyperplanes of each pair. Our custom GJK implementation takes support functions as inputs and returns the witness pair, but is not optimized for computational efficiency. We anticipate that accelerated variants such as GJK++ [38] can further reduce the runtime of GJK-CBF. For the 3D comparison, we consider a 4 × 8 × 1 mbox-shaped robot navigating through a narrow vertical slit formed by two superellipsoidal obstacles (Fig. 1). The robot moves from p0 = [0 , 2.5, 0] ⊤ with an initial atti-tude of η0 = [10 ◦, 0, 0] ⊤ to pd = [20 , 2, 2.8] ⊤ with zero desired attitude. The obstacles are centered at (12 , −4, 2) 

and (12 , 5, 2) with semi-axes (4 .5, 3.6, 7.2) and ρ = 3 . The proposed GJK-CBF uses the original geometries, whereas for the ellipsoid-only baseline [7], we approximate each body by its minimum-volume enclosing ellipsoid. We set kv = 3 ,

kω = 0 .8, W = diag( I3, 3I3), T = 5 s, and ∆t = 1 ms for both methods. As shown in Fig. 1, conservative ellipsoidal approximations block direct passage through the slit, forcing a slight detour above the obstacles (Fig. 1-Top), whereas GJK-CBF uses the original convex bodies, allowing direct passage (Fig. 1-Bottom).

VI. C ONCLUSIONS AND FUTURE WORK 

We present GJK-CBF, a general distance-based CBF for-mulation for collision avoidance between convex bodies, provided that at least one body in each pair is strictly convex. We show that GJK-CBF is continuously differen-tiable and its gradient can be computed solely from the witness pair, without using diffOpt. We validate our method through multi-robot position swapping in both 2D and 3D, a single robot passing through a vertical slit in 3D, and a UR5 manipulator application. Comparative studies with the supporting-hyperplane-CBF [7] show that our method is less conservative while maintaining collision-free motion. Future work will relax the strict-convexity assumption using generalized gradients and nonsmooth CBFs for overlapping and other nondifferentiable configurations and extend the framework to higher-relative-degree systems.

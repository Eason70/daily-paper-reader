# Feedback Dominance Analysis for Pursuit–Evasion Games on Graphs 

Yue Guan 1 Daigo Shishika 2 Dipankar Maity 3 Michael Dorothy 4 Panagiotis Tsiotras 1

Abstract — This work identifies the dominance regions for discrete, simultaneous-move pursuit-evasion games on graphs. Existing geometric approaches provide efficient characteriza-tions of winning regions, but typically provide only sufficient conditions and rely on open-loop strategies. To address these challenges, we develop a set-based dynamic programming approach to characterize the Pursuer’s winning and losing regions, providing necessary and sufficient winning conditions under worst-case behavior. The reachability analysis admits a set-chasing interpretation, allowing translation of dominance sets to feedback strategies that adapt to the players’ positions in real time. For states where neither player can guarantee victory, we introduce an instantaneous matrix-game formulation and establish upper and lower bounds on the Pursuer’s winning probability. Simulation results validate the correctness of the dominance-region characterization and the proposed bounds. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Pursuit–evasion games (PEGs) have been studied exten-sively due to their fundamental role in defense [1], secu-rity [2], and multi-robot systems [3]. In its basic form, a PEG, also known as the cops-and-robbers game [4], considers an Evader attempting to avoid capture by a Pursuer. Many important variants extend this formulation, including reach-avoid games [5] with added evasion states, perimeter-defense 

games [3] with Pursuers restricted to a region’s perimeter, 

target-defense [6] games with turrets aiming at Evaders, etc. A standard approach to solving PEGs is through a game-of-degree formulation, where agents optimize performance metrics such as time-to-capture or time-to-escape. In continu-ous domains, such problems can be formulated as differential games and solved via Hamilton–Jacobi–Isaacs (HJI) equa-tions [7], yielding optimal feedback strategies. In discrete settings, PEGs can be modeled as stochastic games, where Nash equilibria can be computed using value iteration [8]. While these approaches are general and provide guarantees, they do not exploit the underlying geometric structure and are therefore computationally expensive, if not intractable. An alternative line of work leverages geometric structure to obtain efficient solutions for PEGs. In continuous obstacle-free environments, reachability-based constructions such as

This leads to the notion of dominance regions , where, roughly speaking, if the Evader can reach an evasion state strictly earlier than the Pursuer, then successful evasion is guaranteed. Such approaches are computationally efficient and admit closed-form solutions [11]. However, they provide only sufficient 

conditions and typically yield open-loop strategies based on shortest-path comparisons. This limitation becomes critical in environments with obstacles. Even when the Pursuer can reach all evasion states faster than the Evader, it may still fail to guarantee capture due to dilemmas , where committing to protect one evasion state allows the Evader to escape through others. In such cases, geometric shortest-path comparisons become overly conservative and fail to fully capture the strategic interaction. To illustrate this limitation, consider the scenario in Fig. 1. Although the Pursuer is strictly closer to both evasion states, it must commit immediately to a specific direction due to the obstacle, while the Evader can delay its decision until it reaches point A. As a result, the Evader can employ a feedback strategy that guarantees successful evasion by con-ditioning on the Pursuer’s position when the Evader reaches point A. This behavior cannot be captured by existing open-loop geometric analyses, thus motivating the feedback-based dominance analysis proposed in this work. To address these challenges, prior works have introduced additional structural assumptions, such as sequential actions or restricted motion models [12]–[14]. More recent work [15] considers simultaneous-move formulations, but remains lim-ited to grid environments without obstacles. 

Contributions: In this work, we develop a reachability-based framework for simultaneous-move PEGs on general graphs with arbitrary capture regions. We propose a set-based dynamic programming approach to characterize the winning and losing regions of the Pursuer, providing necessary and sufficient conditions under worst-case encounters. This char-acterization admits a natural set-chasing interpretation [16], leading to closed-loop strategies that adapt to the agents’ actions. Furthermore, for states where neither player can arXiv:2610.06186v1 [cs.GT] 5 Oct 2026 guarantee victory, we introduce an instantaneous matrix-game formulation to analyze the resulting gray zone , and derive upper and lower bounds on the Pursuer’s winning probability. Overall, the proposed framework bridges ge-ometric dominance analysis and feedback-based methods, while remaining computationally tractable and applicable to general graph environments. The effectiveness of the pro-posed approach is validated through numerical simulations. II. P ROBLEM FORMULATION 

We study a discrete-time, two-agent pursuit–evasion game (PEG) with discrete state and action spaces. The Pursuer and the Evader move on a shared discrete environment S.Their states at time t are denoted by xt ∈ S and yt ∈ S ,respectively, and their actions by ut ∈ U (xt) and vt ∈ V (yt),where U(xt) and V(yt) are nonempty, state-dependent action sets. We assume deterministic, separable dynamics: 

xt+1 = f (xt, u t), yt+1 = g(yt, v t).

The Pursuer’s capture region is given by Capt( xt) ⊆ S ,and the set of evasion states is T ⊆ S . Capture occurs when 

yt ∈ Capt( xt), whereas successful evasion occurs when yt ∈T \ Capt( xt). The game terminates at the first occurrence of either event. At each nonterminal time step, both players observe the current joint state (xt, y t) and select their actions simultane-ously. Let Π denote the set of admissible Pursuer strategies 

π : S×S → P (U(·)) , where π(u|x, y ) denotes the probability of selecting action u at joint state (x, y ). The Evader’s strat-egy σ and admissible strategy set Σ are defined analogously. We consider a game of kind : the Pursuer wins if capture occurs or if the game continues indefinitely without success-ful evasion; the Evader wins if successful evasion occurs. We say that a Pursuer strategy π guarantees a win from a given initial joint state if it achieves the winning condition with 

probability one against every admissible Evader strategy. In summary, the game is described by the tuple 

PEG = ⟨S , U, V, f, g, Capt , T ⟩ , (1) and we pursue the following research objectives: 

Objective 1: Characterize the dominance region from which the Pursuer can guarantee a win, as well as the region from which the Evader can guarantee finite-time evasion. 

Objective 2: Construct feedback strategies that guarantee the Pursuer’s victory from its dominance region. 

Objective 3: For states outside both dominance regions, derive bounds on Pursuer’s optimal worst-case win rate. III. P URSUER DOMINANCE REGION

Similar to other dynamic programming approaches, we begin with the terminal condition for the DR, which consists of all states xt, given the current Evader state yt, that do not result in an immediate Pursuer defeat. 

Q(yt, 0) = {xt | yt ∈ Capt( xt) ∪ T c}, (4) where T c = S \ T denotes the complement of the set of evasion states. The Evader has two possible escape options: the bottom-left or bottom-right evasion state. To intercept successfully, the Pursuer must exploit its diagonal motion and follow a shortest path. However, the L-shaped obstacle forces the Pursuer to commit to a direction. Since the capture timing is tight, once committed, the Pursuer cannot adjust if the Evader selects the other escape route. Since a competitive game is being played, the agents may naturally randomize their decisions, which leads to probabilistic outcomes. V. F EEDBACK POLICIES FROM THE DOMINANCE SETS 

In this section, we construct feedback policies based on the Q-sets and Z-sets. Given the current state pair (xt, y t),three cases arise: (a) Pursuer dominance: xt ∈ Q∞(yt); the Pursuer can capture or indefinitely prevent evasion; (b) Evader dominance: xt ∈ Z(yt, k ) for some k; the Evader is guaranteed to evade within k steps; (c) Gray zone: xt ∈ G∞(yt); the outcome is undeter-mined/probabilistic. We first examine the first two cases, which admit determin-istic outcomes.

In this work, we developed a reachability-based frame-work for analyzing dominance regions in simultaneous-move pursuit–evasion games on general graphs. Using a set-based dynamic programming approach, we characterized the Pursuer’s winning and losing regions, which lead to closed-loop feedback strategies that overcome limitations of existing open-loop analysis of dominance regions. For gray-zone states, where neither player can guarantee victory, we introduced a matrix-game formulation that provides bounds on the Pursuer’s winning probability. We demonstrate the correctness of the dominance-region characterization and the validity of the proposed gray-zone bounds through numerical simulations. Future work includes extending the framework to multi-agent settings and tightening gray-zone bounds. VIII. A CKNOWLEDGMENT

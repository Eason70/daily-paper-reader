# Safe Receding-Horizon Control of Fixed-Wing Aircraft Using Constrained Approximate Dynamic Programming 

Felipe Arenas-Uribe, Ricardo Gutierrez, and Jesse B. Hoagg 

Abstract — We present a receding-horizon optimal control for fixed-wing aircraft subject to state constraints. These constraints include operational constraints such as altitude, geofencing, and obstacle avoidance, as well as bounds on flight-path angle, roll angle, and airspeed. The state constraints are composed into a single soft-minimum control barrier function (CBF). We use this composite CBF in a constrained-approximate dynamic program to obtain a sequence of analytic closed-form control functions that approximately minimize a quadratic finite-horizon integral cost subject to the CBF constraint. The resulting receding-horizon control is non-myopic in the sense that it approximately optimizes the integral cost while satisfying the state constraint at all times along the entire prediction horizon. We demonstrate constraint satisfaction and performance in simulations of a fixed-wing aircraft navigating an obstacle-filled airspace under wind uncertainty. We also compare this receding-horizon control with 2 other methods. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Over the past decade, uncrewed air vehicles (UAVs) have grown rapidly in deployment and applications. Fixed-wing UAVs are of interest, in large part, because of their energy efficiency, flight range, and cargo capabilities [1]. Applications of fixed-wing UAVs include wild-fire sensing, distributed atmospheric sensing, surveillance and precision agriculture [2]–[4]. As UAVs continue to become more common, there is a need for autonomous and safe operation within the airspace. Standard approaches for fixed-wing aircraft control are based on control loops which regulate the deflection of control surfaces using PID or LQR controllers [5]. As mission requirements become more complex, more state-of-the-art control methods can provide better performance [6]–[8]. However, these control approaches do not directly handle safety and actuator constraints. Thus, constrained control for fixed-wing UAVs remains an important area of research [9]. Constrained control can be formalized as forward invari-ance of a set of states, referred to as a safe set. Methods that address this problem include MPC, reference governors, reachability analysis, and control barrier functions (CBFs). In particular, CBFs have been of interest for aerospace appli-cations due to their rigorous guarantees and computational efficiency [9]. Application of CBF methods as safety-filters for fixed-wing aircraft have been addressed in [4], [10]–[12]. However, this CBF-based optimization is myopic in the sense that the constraint is enforced pointwise in time based only

In [13], the trajectory predicted under a nominal control is used to enforce safety over the horizon; however, the control does not optimize performance over that horizon. Layered CBF architectures [14] and nonlinear MPC with CBF constraints [15], [16] balance safety and performance but can be computationally expensive. Alternatively, predicted-flow CBFs can be used to certify safety over the prediction horizon while simultaneously allowing for performance optimization over the horizon [17], [18]. Another approach is presented in [19] using constrained approximate dynamic programming (C-ADP) and a quadratic approximation of the cost-to-go. This method uses C-ADP combined with a CBF-like constraint to obtain a receding-horizon optimal control that approximately optimizes an integral cost, while guaranteeing that state constraints are satisfied at all times along the entire prediction horizon. This article presents a receding-horizon optimal control for fixed-wing aircraft subject to state constraints. The controller uses a Dubins aircraft model [20], where the state is extended to include aircraft speed, roll, and flight path. In other words, the control inputs are speed rate, roll rate, and flight-path rate. We use barrier functions to encode constraints on flight-path angle, roll angle, airspeed, altitude, geofencing, and obstacle avoidance. A wind estimate and an upper bound on the estimation error are used to construct robust airspeed barrier functions. We compose the barrier functions into a single soft-minimum CBF, which produces a constraint that can guarantee forward invariance of a subset of the safe set [21], [22]. Next, we use the CBF with C-ADP to construct a sequence of analytic closed-form control functions that approximately minimize a finite-horizon integral cost while satisfying the state constraint along the entire prediction hori-zon. We demonstrate constraint satisfaction and performance in simulations of a fixed-wing aircraft navigating an obstacle-filled airspace under uncertain wind, and we compare the proposed control with two alternative methods.

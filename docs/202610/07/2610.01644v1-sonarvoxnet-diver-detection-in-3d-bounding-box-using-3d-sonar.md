## 摘要
辅助人类潜水员的自主水下航行器（AUV）不仅必须持续跟踪潜水员的三维位置，还必须持续跟踪其全身朝向。然而，基于视觉的感知在水下并不可靠，而前视声呐尽管被广泛使用，却会丢失朝向估计所需的仰角信息，从而构成一个根本性限制。最近商业化的三维声呐保留了仰角信息，但会产生稀疏、含噪的回波；现有检测器是为密集LiDAR数据和始终保持直立、仅绕偏航轴旋转的目标（如车辆、行人）构建的，因此无法表示可自由俯仰和横滚的潜水员。为弥补这一空白，我们提出两项贡献。首先，SonarVoxNet将基于体素的编码器和无锚框的基于中心的检测头适配到三维声呐数据，用连续的6D旋转参数化取代传统的仅偏航旋转表示，以预测完整的9自由度有向边界框——据我们所知，这是首个实现该目标的三维声呐潜水员检测器。其次，Diver3D是首个公开的三维声呐数据集，包含在多样化、非直立姿态下的潜水员完整三维朝向标签，采集于天然洞穴潜水地点。通过对骨干网络和检测头进行受控消融实验，我们表明，准确的三维声呐潜水员检测背后的主导因素是仅偏航旋转向完整SO(3)旋转的转变。这一转变显著提高了检测精度并降低了朝向误差。这些结果表明，仅凭三维声呐即可恢复潜水员的全身朝向，为未来潜水员姿态估计和潜水员-机器人交互研究奠定了基础。

## Abstract
Autonomous underwater vehicles (AUVs) assisting human divers must continuously track not only the diver's 3D position but also their full-body orientation. However, vision-based perception is unreliable underwater, and forward-looking sonar -- despite being widely used -- discards the elevation information needed for orientation estimation, posing a fundamental limitation. Recently commercialized 3D sonar preserves elevation but produces sparse, noisy returns, and existing detectors are built for dense LiDAR data and for targets that remain upright and rotate only about the yaw axis (e.g., vehicles, pedestrians), making them unable to represent a freely pitching and rolling diver. To address this gap, we present two contributions. First, SonarVoxNet adapts a voxel-based encoder and an anchor-free center-based detection head to 3D sonar data, replacing the conventional yaw-only rotation representation with a continuous 6D rotation parameterization to predict full 9-DoF oriented bounding boxes -- to our knowledge, the first 3D sonar diver detector to do so. Second, Diver3D is the first public 3D sonar dataset with full 3D orientation labels for divers in diverse, non-upright poses, collected at a natural cave-diving site. Through controlled ablations over the backbone and detection head, we show that the dominant factor behind accurate 3D sonar-based diver detection is the transition from yaw-only rotation to full-SO(3) rotation. This transition substantially improves detection accuracy and reduces orientation error. These results demonstrate that full-body diver orientation is recoverable from 3D sonar alone, laying the groundwork for future work on diver pose estimation and diver-robot interaction.

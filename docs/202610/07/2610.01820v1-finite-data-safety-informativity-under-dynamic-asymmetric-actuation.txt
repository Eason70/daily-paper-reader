# Finite-Data Safety Informativity Under Dynamic Asymmetric Actuation 

Abhinav Sinha, Senior Member, IEEE , Praveen Kumar Ranjan, Member, IEEE ,and Yongcan Cao, Senior Member, IEEE 

Abstract —When the system model is not fully known, mea-surement error and limited excitation can leave several models consistent with the same finite data. A command judged safe for one model may fail for another, while limited control authority can prevent the corrective action needed to pre-serve safety. To ensure safety under model uncertainty and asymmetric input limits, we develop a finite-data certificate that determines whether a command can enforce a prescribed safety inequality. For a linearly parameterized safety channel with exactly known regressors and bounded aggregate residual error, we derive a support formula for the worst-case safety contribution of all data-consistent models. The formula identifies the regressor directions that admit a finite bound, allowing rank-deficient records to contribute to safety certification. Using certified componentwise bounds on actuator tracking error yields an affine inequality with a necessary and sufficient test for pointwise command feasibility. The affine inequality reduces computation of the closest certified command to a scalar root-finding problem. It also yields a closed-form gate that selects the largest certified fraction of a prescribed command segment. The proposed certificate guarantees output safety within its operating domain, provided the feedback is locally Lipschitz and the uncertainty bounds remain valid. Domain retention and full-state continuation extend this guarantee to all time. A vehicle study demonstrates that output safety can be certified from finite measurements in a safety-critical setting with model and actuator uncertainty. 

Index Terms —Data informativity, safety-critical control, dy-namic asymmetric actuation, data-driven control, safe learning. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

In many applications, a controller must meet a performance objective while respecting constraints that define safe opera-tion. A vehicle, for example, may need to follow a reference trajectory while remaining inside a moving corridor. Near the corridor boundary, the controller must retain enough authority to counter disturbances without violating the input limits. When control decisions rely on finite measurements, the data must support the safety of a proposed correction, and the actuators must be able to realize it. Set-invariance theory and control barrier functions pro-vide conditions for selecting corrective inputs that keep trajectories within admissible sets [1], [2]. Safety filters use these conditions to modify a nominal command through

Higher-order barrier designs address constraints of high relative degree [4] and the feasibility of corrective inputs under tight input bounds [5]. These safety guarantees depend on a model or uncertainty bounds that remain valid during operation. When a reliable model is unavailable, unknown contributions from the dynamics prevent direct evaluation of these conditions. Measurements can be used to estimate the unknown terms and quantify the uncertainty relevant to safety certification. Gaussian-process methods use prediction bounds to account for uncertainty in barrier conditions [6], [7], while rein-forcement learning can estimate corrections to the model terms entering safety constraints [8]. Conformal calibration connects learned models to probabilistic guarantees [9]. Ro-bust adaptive and uncertainty-estimation methods combine estimates with certified error bounds [10], [11]. However, accurate prediction and uncertainty quantification do not establish whether a given finite record is sufficient for safety certification under limited control authority. A deterministic certificate based on a finite record must establish that an admissible command satisfies the safety inequality for every model consistent with the measurements and their error bound. Data informativity formalizes this question of sufficiency by assessing whether a record supports a specified task for all data-consistent systems [12], [13]. In linear feedback syn-thesis, data parameterizations and matrix inequalities yield conditions for such sufficiency under bounded errors [14]– [16]. For nonlinear systems, set-membership and barrier-learning methods use structural and regularity assumptions to obtain deterministic guarantees from data [17], [18]. A further challenge is to preserve the safety guarantee when the command is realized by a dynamic actuator. Tracking error can invalidate the safety inequality, while asymmetric limits restrict the corrective action available to maintain safety. Actuator-aware barrier formulations address this challenge through optimization-based filters that account for dynam-ically generated inputs or unmodeled input dynamics [19], [20]. Admissibility-preserving input realization (APIR) em-beds asymmetric limits directly in a smooth actuator map, keeping the realized input admissible by construction [21]– [23]. Within this realization, certified tracking-error bounds quantify a margin in the safety inequality that accounts for the difference between commanded and realized inputs. arXiv:2610.01820v1 [eess.SY] 1 Oct 2026 Output-safety certification from finite data still faces an obstacle in the sense that the corrective action required by the data-consistent models may exceed the authority available under actuator tracking error and asymmetric input limits. Addressing this problem requires bounding the safety contri-bution of the data-consistent models without full parameter identification and comparing that bound with the authority available under actuator tracking error. In such scenarios, a feasible certificate must be converted into command-selection rules with the regularity needed for trajectory safety. We ad-dress these requirements through the following contributions.  

> •

We characterize the minimum residual-error budget for data consistency and quantify the amplification of uncer-tainty at each queried regressor. For a consistent record and a nonzero safety functional, the result identifies the unmeasured regressor directions that make the worst-case model contribution unbounded.  

> •

We quantify the loss of certified control authority due to actuator tracking error as an additive reserve. Sub-tracting this reserve from the static finite-data authority characterizes pointwise feasibility for the chosen certifi-cate and recovers the static certificate when the error bounds vanish.  

> •

For implementation, we derive the command projection and admission gate with sufficient conditions for local Lipschitz continuity. A counterexample establishes that pointwise certificate feasibility can hold even when no continuous feedback selector exists.  

> •

We validate the certificate numerically on a vehicle model by comparing coverage across equal-sized mea-surement records and against static and exact-parameter benchmarks. Closed-loop checks assess output and input constraint satisfaction, and halving the integration step preserves sampled certificate feasibility. II. P ROBLEM FORMULATION

We developed a finite-data framework for certifying non-linear output safety under dynamic asymmetric actuation. The measured regressor span characterized the queries admitting finite support for nonzero safety functionals without prior parameter bounds. Combining data support with a certified actuator-error tube yielded a pointwise feasibility condition, a minimum-deviation command, and a maximal gate along a prescribed command segment. Under the stated regularity and continuation conditions, the feedback preserved output and input constraints within the certification domain. The vehicle study indicated that measurement selection from a broader flight campaign tightened certificates and increased coverage despite equal record sizes and full row rank. The reported response maintained tracking and velocity bounds, with non-positive certificate margins at sampled command updates. The study motivated acquisition strategies balancing certifi-cate improvement against sampling effort and augmented-state certificates that retained actuator dynamics to reduce conservatism.

Abstract 

Safety filters for large-scale multi-agent systems can be formulated at the population level through mean-field control barrier functions (MF-CBFs). Most existing formulations are first-order, requiring the control input to appear explicitly in the first time derivative of the safety functional; higher-order extensions have only very recently been proposed for a single, directly actuated population. We consider instead the case in which the population to be kept safe is not directly actuated, but is transported by a field generated by a second, controlled population, possibly in the presence of diffusion. For this setting, we develop a higher-order MF-CBF framework for density dynamics with safety constraints of relative degree larger than one. Our main result is that indirect actuation of this kind does not destroy the control-affine structure of the safety condition: the control-dependent part of the second time derivative of the safety functional is obtained in closed form and is linear in the control velocity field, with a kernel given by the actuated density times the spatial gradient of a suitable first variation. Relative-degree-two safety constraints can therefore still be enforced through a convex quadratic program. We then specialize the framework to a leader–follower density control problem in the presence of dangerous regions, where safety is ensured by keeping the overlap of each population with those regions below a prescribed threshold. We show that the follower safety functional has relative degree two with respect to the leader control field, while the leader safety functional has relative degree one. We further characterize exactly when the two simultaneous constraints are infeasible, introduce an adaptive-gain mechanism to mitigate potential conflicts, and bound the tracking errors induced by the safety correction. Numerical simulations in cluttered environments illustrate the resulting safety-performance trade-off. 

Key words: Control barrier functions and state space constraints; Infinite-dimensional multi-agent systems and networks; Multi-agent systems; Large-scale complex systems; Large-scale and networked optimization problems. 

1 Introduction 

Leader–follower control provides scalable strategies for co-ordinating large populations of agents by acting on a small set of leaders, whose dynamics indirectly shapes the collec-tive behavior of followers through local interactions (Cruz, 1978; Tanner et al., 2004; Cao et al., 2012; Notarstefano et al., 2011). This control problem arises in many applica-tions where only a fraction of the agents can be directly controlled. Examples include a small number of connected autonomous vehicles regulating the flow of surrounding human-driven traffic (Stern et al., 2018; Siri et al., 2021); robot or drone swarms in search-and-rescue operations, where a few informed units guide the rest of the swarm to-

In all these settings, direct control of all agents is infeasible. A paradigmatic example is shep-herding (Str¨ ombom et al., 2014; Lama and di Bernardo, 2024; Lama et al., 2025), where a small number of leaders (herders) is used to steer a larger population of followers (targets) toward a desired region. Shepherding has also re-cently been investigated in unstructured environments which make indirect steering and safety preservation particularly challenging (Tomaselli et al., 2026a; Punzo et al., 2025). Recent continuum formulations recast such problems in the space of probability distributions within the scope of leader-follower density control (Maffettone et al., 2025; Salzano et al., 2026), helping to mitigate the curse of dimensional-ity and enabling rigorous results on convergence, robustness and performance. However, existing leader-follower den-

Enforcing such constraints is challenging because the agents to be kept safe may not be directly actuated: in leader-follower systems, the safety of the followers must be guaranteed by acting only on the leaders, that is, at the macroscopic level, through the control of the leader density. Control barrier functions (CBFs) represent a powerful tool to enforce safety constraints in real time for nonlinear con-trol systems (Ames et al., 2016, 2019). By imposing suit-able differential inequalities on a safety function, CBFs pro-vide constraints that make a prescribed safe set forward in-variant. This framework has been extended in several di-rections, including stochastic CBFs (S-CBFs) (Clark, 2021; Wang et al., 2021a,b), which allow safety filters to be im-posed on stochastic dynamics, exponential CBFs (Nguyen and Sreenath, 2016) and high-order CBFs (HO-CBFs) (Xiao and Belta, 2021; Breeden and Panagou, 2023), which ad-dress safety constraints with relative degree larger than one, and adaptive CBFs (A-CBFs) (Xiao et al., 2021; Parwana and Panagou, 2025), which improve feasibility by tuning barrier parameters online when safety requirements become restrictive or potentially conflicting. Related developments have also addressed multi-agent safety through non-smooth barrier functions, enabling Boolean compositions of multi-ple system-level constraints in multi-robot systems (Glotfel-ter et al., 2017). At the population level, mean-field control barrier functions (MF-CBFs) (Fung and Nurbekyan, 2025) have recently been introduced to enforce safety constraints directly on density dynamics. These methods extend the classical CBF frame-work to mean-field models and provide safety filters for large-scale multi-agent systems. Recent extensions include Banach-space control barrier functions (Gao et al., 2026) which generalize control barrier functions to Banach spaces, and mean-field control barrier functions for stochastic multi-agent systems (Tomaselli et al., 2026b), whose mean-field evolution is described by advection-diffusion equations. In the latter setting, to solve a shepherding problem, leader-follower interactions were considered under the assumption that the follower velocity field could be directly adjusted by the safety filter. However, in many leader-follower systems, followers are not directly actuated: their motion is shaped only indirectly through the controlled leader population. The first-order MF-CBF formulations in (Fung and Nurbekyan, 2025; Tomaselli et al., 2026b) are therefore insufficient for this setting, since they require the control input to appear explicitly in the first time derivative of the safety functional. Concurrently with and independently of the present work, higher-order MF-CBFs have been proposed in (Nurbekyan and Fung, 2026). That work defines the relative degree of a mean-field safety functional with respect to control-affine individual-agent dynamics ˙x = f + gu, provides a general calculus for its higher-order derivatives along the corresponding continuity equation, and applies it to cross-and self-correlation barriers for double-integrator swarms, where position-only constraints have relative degree two. In (Nurbekyan and Fung, 2026), the constrained density and the actuated density coincide: relative degree two arises because the control acts on the velocity component of the agents’ state, and the dynamics is deterministic. Here, we address a structurally different mechanism, in which the agents to be kept safe belong to a different, non-actuated 

population, whose density obeys advection-diffusion dy-namics and is affected by the control only through a nonlo-cal interaction field generated by the actuated population. This cross-population, indirect actuation is not covered by the single-population framework of (Nurbekyan and Fung, 2026), and it raises issues that do not appear there, namely the coexistence of constraints of different relative degree on the leader and follower populations and their possible mutual conflict, as well as the effect of the safety correction on the follower tracking performance. This paper addresses this gap by developing a higher-order MF-CBF framework for safe indirect density control. As a common setting, we first recall the notion of relative degree of a mean-field safety functional and the recursive barrier condition guaranteeing forward invariance of the safe set, along the lines of finite-dimensional HO-CBFs (Xiao and Belta, 2021) and consistently with (Nurbekyan and Fung, 2026). Building on this setting, the main contributions of the paper are the following. First, we show that indirect actuation through a transported density does not destroy the control-affine structure of the safety condition. Specifically, for a density ρ whose evolu-tion is driven by a field generated by an actuated density q,we obtain in closed form the decomposition ¨H = I(ρ, q) + ⟨Ψ(ρ, q), u⟩, Ψ = q ∇∂ fq ˙H ,

with both terms computable from the model. This is what makes the higher-order mean-field condition usable in prac-tice: although the actuation is indirect and the dynamics infinite-dimensional, the resulting relative-degree-two con-straint remains affine in the control velocity field, so that the safety filter reduces to a convex quadratic program. The re-sult holds for constrained densities with diffusion and nonlo-cal interactions, and extends to networks of interacting popu-lations, where the relative degree is determined by the graph distance from the actuated population to the constrained one, with the same affine structure. Second, we specialize the framework to a leader-follower density control problem in cluttered environments. We show that the follower safety functional has relative degree two with respect to the leader control input, since the followers are not directly actuated but are transported by an interaction field generated by the leader density. Conversely, the leader safety functional has relative degree one. Safety constraints of different relative degree on the two populations are thus enforced through a single convex quadratic program acting on the leader control field. 2Third, we give a necessary and sufficient condition for the feasibility of simultaneous leader-follower safety con-straints and introduce an adaptive-gain mechanism, inspired by adaptive CBFs, to mitigate potential conflicts between the two safety requirements. Finally, we quantify how the correction applied by the safety filter propagates to the leader and follower tracking errors. Numerical simulations in cluttered environments illustrate the resulting safety-performance trade-off, and show that en-forcing leader safety alone through a first-order MF-CBF does not preserve follower safety, whereas the proposed higher-order filter does. The interaction between safety constraints of different rela-tive degree on distinct populations, their feasibility, and their effect on tracking performance have no counterpart in the single-population setting of (Nurbekyan and Fung, 2026). The rest of this paper is structured as follows. Sec. 2 re-calls notation and mathematical preliminaries; Sec. 3 intro-duces the leader–follower model and the problem statement; Sec. 4 develops the HO-MF-CBF framework; Sec. 5 applies the results to leader-follower safety, analyzes feasibility, and provides a boundedness result for the tracking error; Sec. 6 validates the methodology through numerical simulations, and Sec. 7 concludes the work.

However, in many systems, the control affects the state only indirectly and therefore does not appear explicitly in ˙H . To formalize this scenario, we introduce the notion of relative degree. 

Definition 2 (Relative degree) Consider the density dy-namics (12) and let H be a continuously differentiable functional. We say that H has relative degree r with re-spect to the control input u if r is the smallest integer such that the r-th total time derivative of H , denoted by H (r),depends explicitly on u; that is, the coefficient multiplying 

u in H (k) vanishes identically for all admissible states and all k < r, whereas for k = r it does not vanish identically. 

The relative degree is thus a structural property of the pair 

(H , F ), in the same sense as in the finite-dimensional case and in (Nurbekyan and Fung, 2026). It identifies the deriva-tive in which the control can enter, but it does not guarantee that the control coefficient is nonzero at every state reached along a given evolution. 7 Conclusion 

In this work, we proposed a Higher-Order Mean-Field Con-trol Barrier Function framework for indirectly actuated den-sity systems, complementing the single-population higher-order MF-CBFs developed concurrently in (Nurbekyan and Fung, 2026). By accounting for safety functionals with rel-ative degree larger than one, the proposed construction en-ables the enforcement of safety constraints on populations that cannot be directly controlled. As in the HO-CBF frame-work, we established a recursive higher-order mean-field barrier condition and showed that, under suitable regularity and initial feasibility assumptions, it guarantees forward in-variance of the corresponding safe set. Our main technical result is that, when a density is transported by a field gen-erated by a second actuated density, the control-dependent part of the second time derivative of the safety functional 15 t

Indirect actuation therefore raises the relative degree of the safety functional without sacrific-ing the convexity of the associated safety filter. We applied our framework to a leader-follower density con-trol problem, where the follower population is not directly actuated and its safety must be enforced through the con-trolled leader density. In this setting, we showed that the follower safety functional has relative degree two with re-spect to the leader control field, whereas the leader safety functional has relative degree one. This naturally leads to a safety filter defined by affine constraints in the leader con-trol input. We also discussed feasibility issues that arise when safety constraints are imposed simultaneously on leader and fol-lower populations. In particular, we showed that conflicts may arise between the two barrier conditions and introduced an adaptive-gain mechanism to mitigate such conflicts. Nu-merical simulations in cluttered environments illustrated that the proposed HO-MF-CBF filter can preserve follower safety during the transient phase, while highlighting the safety-performance trade-off. Future work will address feasibility under bounded control inputs, a systematic treatment of general networks of inter-acting populations, robust safety in the presence of uncertain or time-varying obstacles, and the derivation of finite-agent safety guarantees from mean-field barrier conditions.

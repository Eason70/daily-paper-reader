# Safe Ergodic Control for Multi-Robot Systems via Quadratic Programming 

Yo Toyomoto 1, Mohamed Elobaid 1, Bryce L. Ferguson 2, Alberto Quattrini Li 3 and Shinkyu Park 1

Abstract — Ergodic control drives robots to spend time in each region in proportion to a spatial distribution of interest, making it well suited for dense spatiotemporal environmental monitoring. Existing safe ergodic controllers rely on offline tra-jectory optimization or hierarchical architectures, which limit real-time applicability and decouple the ergodicity objective from the safety constraint. This paper presents a quadratic programming (QP)-based controller that treats ergodicity and safety jointly. We first introduce a Gaussian-kernel ergodic metric that, unlike the classical indicator-based metric, is time differentiable. This allows the exponential decay of the metric to be imposed as a time-varying control barrier function (CBF) constraint, relaxed by a slack variable, alongside hard CBF constraints for region containment and inter-robot collision avoidance. We establish that the resulting QP remains feasible from any safe initial configuration and that, up to the slack term, the ergodic metric decays exponentially. Simulations show improved performance over baseline methods, and experiments with three aerial vehicles validate the work on real hardware. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Employing multiple mobile sensors, such as underwater robots and aerial vehicles with onboard sensors, yields far denser spatiotemporal data over a vast field than manual sampling [1]. A key challenge, however, lies in operating these robots efficiently; in particular, adapting their motion to an area of interest is critical to monitoring performance (e.g., through assigning a probability density function as in search and rescue scenarios [2], or mimicking heat maps cor-responding to industrial inspection). In this context, coverage path planning has been widely studied, seeking the shortest path that covers the field [3], [4]. Coverage control has also been applied to distribute multiple robots such that their stationary spatial distribution matches the area of interest [5], [6]. However, neither approach directly optimizes the time history of the robots’ positions to match the spatial distribution required for monitoring the area. Ergodic control instead matches the robots’ trajectories to the target spatial distribution. Because this metric is difficult to use directly in controller design, they proposed the spectral mul-tiscale coverage (SMC) method, which approximates both distributions using Fourier series and derives a closed-form control input that minimizes the discrepancy between their Fourier coefficients. Fourier series-based ergodic control has since been widely studied [8]–[15], and alternative approxi-mations of the ergodic metric, e.g., using heat equations [16], kernel functions [17], sample-based kernel embedding [18] and optimization methods, e.g., learning methods [19], and flow matching methods [20], have been explored. However, none of these methods guarantee safety, such as collision avoidance between robots. Several studies incorporate safety constraints in ergodic trajectory optimization [21]–[23], but, to the best of our knowledge, all of them are executed offline or rely on a long optimization horizon, which makes them difficult to adapt online. Hierarchical approaches that run SMC in an upper layer and a control barrier function (CBF)-based safety filter in a lower layer have also been proposed [24], [25]. This hierarchical structure, however, cannot guarantee decay of the ergodic metric as the safety filter may degrade ergodicity convergence. This paper presents a single-layer quadratic programming (QP)-based controller that decreases the ergodic metric while ensuring safety. To this end, we first define an ergodic metric using a Gaussian kernel which renders the metric time differentiable. This metric definition also differs from [17] in that we apply the Gaussian filter to all terms of Equation arXiv:2610.05258v1 [cs.RO] 4 Oct 2026 (23) in [17], which more accurately captures the similarity of the trajectory to the target distribution. This property allows us to design the controller with a constraint-driven approach [26], which can be executed online in real time from the current state. Specifically, the decay rate of the ergodic metric is imposed as a constraint that is affine in the control input, structured as a time-varying CBF [27], so that additional safety-critical constraints can be added as CBFs by construction. Unlike hierarchical approaches [24], [25], the control input is optimized by considering the ergodic-metric decay and the safety constraints simultaneously. Simulations compare the decay of the ergodic metric under the proposed controller against the SMC and hierarchical SMC-CBF meth-ods, and experiments with multiple Crazyflies, as shown in Figure 1, validate the controller design. The rest of the paper is organized as follows. Notations are introduced below. Section II introduces a novel ergodic met-ric using a Gaussian kernel, and the control problem is for-mulated. Section III presents a controller design based on a CBF-based QP approach. Section IV validates the controller through simulations across various scenarios against baseline methods, and further experimental results with Crazyflies are presented. Section V concludes the paper and discusses directions for future work.

In all simulations, Q is a cube with side length 10 m

step as the grid, with σmin = 0 .1 m equal to the grid spacing. Performance is sensitive to σmax , particularly when it is too small, so its value is chosen by trading off computational load against the diminishing performance gain from larger values. The decay rate γ is chosen heuristically: a large γ

makes the condition h(t) ≥ 0 hard to satisfy at all times, whereas a smaller γ prescribes slow decay. We start from a large γ and reduce it, examining the decay of the ergodic metric over the first 20 s .The robot positions and ¯κ are sampled every 0.01 s and accumulated into d(t, q, σ ), with the metric initialized at 

t0 = 0 .01 s from p(t0). Since evaluating the ergodic metric and its derivatives is comparatively computationally expen-sive, they are computed every 0.2 s , and each evaluation triggers one QP solve. The QP is solved using the Python 

osqp package [29] with 5 polishing iterations, absolute and relative tolerances of 10 −3, and at most 10 6 iterations. If the solver reports primal infeasibility, the problem is re-solved without the input constraint (16) (cf. Remark 1). C. Drones Experiments 

We validate the proposed controller on three aerial drones, Crazyflie. A Qualisys motion-capture system provides posi-tion measurements, from which ¯κ is evaluated every 0.01 s on a central computer; the same measurements are fed back to the drones for local control. The central computer solves the QP and transmits u⋆j via radio communication to each drone as a velocity reference, which the onboard PID controller tracks. The flight volume is [−2.0 m , 2.0 m] in x and y and 

[0 .2 m , 1.2 m] in z, with the floor at z = 0 .0 m . We consider the form (18) with nφ ∈ {2, 3, 4} peaks, σφ = 0.12 ,and peak centers qφi drawn at random at least 4σφ away from the boundary. The maximum velocity of the robot is 

umax = 0 .2 m /s. The boundary CBF uses βb(θ) = 0 .9 θ,chosen so that the QP remains feasible at takeoff from the floor, i.e., outside the safe set. The collision-avoidance CBF uses rmin = 0.3 m and βc(θ) = 0.25 θ, chosen so that physical contact, which occurs at a separation of about 0.1 m ,is avoided even in the worst case. and σmax = 2 .0 is smaller than that between σmax = 0 .3

and σmax = 1 .0, consistent with the weaker dependence of 

ρj on σ at larger scales. Since the cost of evaluating the ergodic metric and its time derivatives grows with σmax , we conclude that σmax should be large enough to avoid myopic behavior, beyond which the choice trades computational cost against more direct transitions between peaks. V. C ONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORKS 

We have presented a single-layer ergodic controller in QP form. A Gaussian-kernel ergodic metric was introduced that, unlike the classical indicator-based metric, is differentiable in time, which allows the decay of the metric to be imposed as a time-varying CBF constraint alongside hard CBF constrains for region containment and collision avoidance. The simu-lations showed ergodicity comparable to SMC for a single robot and substantially better ergodicity than a hierarchical SMC-CBF baseline for multiple robots. The experiments with three Crazyflies confirmed ergodic behavior with safe operation on real hardware. Future work includes distributed controller design, adapta-tion of time-varying target distributions, extension to higher-order dynamics with sensor models, and validation in more cluttered environments, all of which are needed for deploy-ment in real environmental monitoring.

# LBA-CBF : Rapidly Adaptive Safety Filters via Parallel Dynamics Inference 

Maitham F. AL-Sunni Timeea-Andreea Radu Hassan Almubarak Henry Z. Liao Michael Görner Francesco Maurelli John M. Dolan 

Abstract — Control barrier functions (CBFs) certify com-mands through an assumed dynamics model, so an abrupt, un-measured regime change can undermine the certificate exactly when safety matters most. We present Look-Back Adaptive Control Barrier Functions ( LBA-CBF ), which rank a finite bank of candidate dynamics by recent prediction error over a short look-back window and enforce the high-order CBF condition against every model within a tolerance of the best, spanning best-fit adaptation to full-bank robust filtering. The dynamics may depend nonlinearly on the unknown parameters, and no switching model or continuously parameterized estimator is required. We prove that any feasible filtered input satisfies the true CBF condition whenever a safety-representative candidate is retained. In quadrotor simulation with abrupt wind reversals and an unknown payload, LBA-CBF is safe and reaches the goal from all random initial conditions, matching an oracle, while adaptive and robust baselines achieve 0–88% success. Banks of up to 250 , 000 models run inside the control loop, and Crazyflie 2.1 and F1TENTH experiments demonstrate adaptation to wind, payload release, and varying tire–road friction. Code, videos, and project details are available at: https://lla-control.github.io 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

A control safety filter is only as trustworthy as the dynamics model used inside its barrier condition. Control barrier functions (CBF) [1] keep the state inside a safe set with minimal deviation from a nominal controller, but certify each input through the predicted evolution of the barrier under an assumed dynamics model. An abrupt, unmeasured change of dynamics regime , such as an unknown payload, a sudden wind gust, or a change in tire–road friction, therefore undermines the filter exactly when safety matters most. Two main remedies exist. Robust CBFs certify the barrier against a fixed worst-case uncertainty set [2], which can be conservative in a way that online data cannot reduce. Adaptive and learning-based CBFs reduce this conservatism by estimating unknown parameters or model errors online, but abrupt regime changes must be absorbed through an esti-mation transient. We propose Look-Back Adaptive Control Barrier Func-tions ( LBA-CBF ), whose architecture is illustrated in Fig. 1. Rather than committing the safety filter to a single contin-uously evolving estimate, LBA-CBF maintains a finite bank of candidate dynamics models and, at every control step, evaluates them in parallel by their prediction error over a look-back window of length W . The best-fitting model can be used directly for rapid adaptation, while a prediction-plausible set retains models whose recent errors lie within a tolerance of the minimum and enforces the CBF condition for all retained models. After W new transitions, pre-change observations no longer contribute to the residual, so the filter adapts to changes without a switching model. 

A. Related Work 1) Robust and adaptive safety: CBFs have been ex-tended to high-relative-degree and discrete-time safety con-straints [4, 5]. Under model uncertainty, robust CBFs certify safety against a prescribed uncertainty set [2], while adaptive arXiv:2610.08765v1 [cs.RO] 6 Oct 2026 CBFs reduce conservatism through online parameter adapta-tion [6]. Robust adaptive CBFs (RaCBFs) combine adapta-tion with set-membership identification [3], with extensions to high-relative-degree constraints [7]. Alternatively, safety can be embedded into an adaptive controller by augmenting the parametrically uncertain dynamics with barrier states [8]. More recent approaches accommodate time-varying uncer-tainty: robust CBFs in [9] admit time-varying parameters that enter the dynamics nonlinearly and use set-membership es-timation to reduce conservatism, while [10] combines finite-time estimation of time-varying parameters with RaCBFs under a structured parameterization and bounded parameter variation. Disturbance-observer [11] and L1 [12] approaches instead estimate and compensate model mismatch through observer or adaptive dynamics. In contrast, LBA-CBF does not require a continuously parameterized estimator or an affine-in-parameter uncertainty model: it ranks a finite bank of candidate dynamics directly from recent prediction error and certifies the barrier against the dynamics hypotheses retained by the moving look-back window. 

2) Switched dynamics and regime change: CBF meth-ods for hybrid and switched systems construct mode-dependent safety conditions under a specified hybrid struc-ture [13], while learning-based approaches have estimated mode-specific barrier residuals for switching systems [14]. In contrast, LBA-CBF treats the active dynamics regime as latent and infers it from recent state–action transitions without requiring known guards or a transition model. 

3) Multiple-model methods: Adaptation through a finite bank of candidate dynamics is a classical multiple-model control idea [15]. More recently, multiple-model reasoning has appeared in safety-critical control: [16] combines CBF constraints with MPC for jump Markov linear systems under uncertain mode estimates, while [17] uses a supervisory multi-observer bank to estimate unknown parameters within an adaptive safety controller. Most closely related to our inference mechanism, the look-back bank of [18, 19] ranks candidate vehicle models over a short window and selects the best-fitting model for adaptive racing model predictive control (MPC). LBA-CBF instead uses the windowed predic-tion residual for safety certification, retaining the best-fitting dynamics or a prediction-plausible subset and enforcing the high-order CBF (HOCBF) against them.

The proposed LBA-CBF framework consists of three stages. First, a finite bank of candidate dynamics models is constructed over the operating regimes. Second, a look-back module evaluates how well each candidate explains the most recent transitions and retains a set of prediction-plausible models. Third, the safety filter enforces the CBF condition against every retained model and returns the admissible input closest to the nominal command.

2) Baselines and metrics: We evaluate three adaptive CBF filters and one robust CBF filter, alongside fixed-model and oracle references. The disturbance-observer CBF (DOB-CBF) [11] low-pass filters the nominal model’s planar force residual at bandwidth 33 s −1. The L1 adaptive CBF (L1-AC) [12] estimates the same force using a predictor-based L1 adaptation law and a 33 rad /s low-pass filter. The high-order robust adaptive CBF (HO-RaCBF) [7] updates the force estimate by gradient descent within a priori bounds 

|wx|, |wy | ≤ 0.315 N , with a robustness margin that contracts with excitation. The fixed-model Nominal CBF assumes θ = 0,whereas the Oracle receives the true θk at every step. The three adaptive baselines use additive planar-force uncertainty models, which support their observer constructions and the linear-in-parameter formulation of HO-RaCBF. These imple-mentations do not explicitly identify the payload-dependent input gain. B. Quadrotor: Hardware Experiments 

We deploy LBA-CBF on a Crazyflie 2.1 [24] tracked by a motion-capture system. An LQR running at 50 Hz 

produces the nominal horizontal accelerations unom , which the safety filter modifies so that the vehicle avoids a circular obstacle (Fig. 5). The filter uses (20) in the horizontal plane around an upright cylinder: no gravity term, no drag, and the commanded horizontal accelerations in place of the rotor thrusts. The unknown parameters are the planar external forces θ = ( wx, w y ), used for both wind and payload. The bank is a uniform 500 × 500 grid over (wx, w y ),

M = 250 , 000 , spanning [−0.2, 0.2] 2 N in Experiments 1 and 2 and [−0.1, 0.1] 2 N in Experiment 3, where the payload produces smaller forces; a grid covers the unknown wind and payload intensities uniformly, with a look-back window 

W = 3 throughout. In all experiments, we set η = 0,selecting the best-fit endpoint of LBA-CBF , which keeps a smaller set of barrier constraints, allowing us to isolate the look-back adaptation mechanism while minimizing the online optimization cost. 

Experiment 1 (hover under wind). The vehicle hovers beside the obstacle while the fan pushes it toward the unsafe set; see Fig. 4(A). With the Nominal CBF, the vehicle hovered for only approximately 8 s before crashing into the obstacle. With LBA-CBF , it hovered for more than twice as long with the fan running, and h remained at or above zero. 

Experiment 2 (flight to a goal under wind). The vehicle flies past the obstacle to a goal while the fan pushes it toward the obstacle; see Fig. 4(B). Across eight rep-etitions, LBA-CBF kept h ≥ 0 throughout, whereas the Nominal CBF crossed h = 0 in six of the eight runs shown. 

Experiment 3 (hover with a swinging payload). A mass hangs from the vehicle on a rope; see Fig. 4(C). Because the payload swings, the disturbance never settles, so the filter must follow a continuously changing regime rather than converge to one. After confirming that LBA-CBF maintained safety in this condition, we cut the rope in flight, imposing a second, abrupt change of the dynamics. The same test was not possible with the Nominal CBF, which had already violated the constraint with the payload attached. 

C. Safe Extreme Driving: Hardware Experiments 

We deploy LBA-CBF on an F1TENTH autonomous rac-ing platform as the model used inside a CBF-constrained MPC, and compare it with the same controller using a fixed nominal model identified on rubber tires. As in [19], the vehicle follows a single-track dynamic bicycle model with Fiala tire forces [25], parameterized by the front and rear cornering stiffnesses and friction coefficients, θ =[Cf , C r , μ f , μ r ]⊤. The bank contains 10 000 models over 

Cf ∈ [175 , 325] N /rad , Cr ∈ [150 , 300] N /rad , and 

μf , μ r ∈ [0 .1, 1.1] , and is evaluated in JAX with η = 0 .The same bank is used for both tire conditions, while the nominal model remains fixed. The MPC is implemented in acados [26] with horizon P = 20 and dt = 0.04 s ,and runs at 25 Hz . We report model quality as the P -step prediction error: the deviation between the state obtained by propagating a model over the MPC horizon under the applied inputs and the measured state. 

Experiment 1 (plastic tires, adaptive drifting) : With 

LBA-CBF , the car is able to stabilize over 6 laps even with consistent tire slippage with minimal conservatism (Fig. 6). The nominal model is overconservative in its optimization, causing it to deviate significantly from the track and crash.

V. C ONCLUSIONS 

We introduced LBA-CBF , a safety filter that uses recent prediction error to decide which dynamics hypotheses the barrier must be certified against: a finite bank is ranked over a look-back window, and a single tolerance spans best-fit adaptation and full-bank robust filtering. In simulation and on Crazyflie 2.1 and F1TENTH hardware, it adapts at control rate to abrupt wind reversals, an unmodeled payload, and changes of tire, where fixed-model, robust, and continuously adapting filters become unsafe or overly conservative. The guarantee remains conditional on bank adequacy and on filter feasibility; constructive conditions for these, and online adaptation of the tolerance, are left to future work. REFERENCES

# Fast Last-Iterate Convergence in Zero-Sum Markov Games with Bandit Feedback 

Yuheng Zhang University of Illinois Urbana-Champaign 

yuhengz2@illinois.edu 

Abstract 

We study last-iterate convergence in unknown two-player zero-sum discounted Markov games with bandit feedback. The players learn independently along a single trajectory without observing each other’s actions. We develop Adaptive Regularized TD Learning (ARTD), which achieves a 

˜O(t−1/4) duality gap bound for the current policies under a uniform hitting time assumption, with high probability simultaneously over all rounds and starting states. This improves the 

˜O(t−1/(9+ ν)) rate of Cai et al. (2023), for any fixed ν > 0, under the same feedback model and hitting time assumption. Our algorithm requires no knowledge of the hitting time bound, the time horizon, or the confidence level. To stabilize policy learning as value estimates change, we separate fast temporal difference averaging from bounded value updates. We adapt log-barrier regularization to the progress of value estimation, controlling both policy and value errors throughout learning. Together, these mechanisms enable fast convergence of the policies actually played, even when the players learn independently from bandit feedback. 

# 1 Introduction 

Markov games provide a standard framework for multi-agent reinforcement learning, in which agents’ decisions jointly determine rewards and state transitions (Shapley, 1953; Littman, 1994). They model competitive tasks such as board games and real-time strategy games, where each player’s actions affect both players’ immediate payoffs and the evolution of the game (Wei et al., 2021b). We study last-iterate convergence in unknown two-player zero-sum discounted Markov games. The players interact along a single trajectory: at each round, they choose actions at the current state, receive their own payoffs, and observe the next state. Both the reward function and transition dynamics are unknown. Each player learns independently from its own observations, without observing the opponent’s actions or policies and without communication or shared randomness. This uncoupled learning model asks whether individually implemented learning rules can lead the two players to a Nash equilibrium (Wei et al., 2021b; Sayin et al., 2021; Cai et al., 2023). Many sample-efficient methods for learning Markov games provide convergence guarantees for averages or mixtures of past policies (Bai et al., 2020; Jin et al., 2024). However, these guarantees do not imply that the policies actually played converge to equilibrium. Achieving this stronger guarantee, known as last-iterate convergence , is challenging because each player must learn from noisy feedback against a changing opponent, without averaging away fluctuations in its policies. Prior work establishes last-iterate guarantees under several feedback models. With full gradient feedback, optimistic and extragradient methods converge in matrix games and related saddle point problems (Daskalakis and Panageas, 2019; Wei et al., 2021a; Cai et al., 2022). Under bandit feedback, Fiegel et al. (2026) obtain a ˜O(t−1/4) high-probability rate for uncoupled learning in matrix games 1arXiv:2610.05968v1 [cs.LG] 5 Oct 2026 using log-barrier regularization. Achieving the same rate in Markov games is substantially more challenging: players must learn policies while estimating future rewards from a single trajectory that visits states unevenly. For Markov games with full information about rewards and transitions, entropy-regularized policy optimization also gives finite-time last-iterate guarantees (Cen et al., 2021, 2023). For unknown Markov games, decentralized methods based on observed rewards and state transitions provide guarantees for the final policies of batched or nested learning procedures (Wei et al., 2021b; Chen et al., 2024). Our work is most closely related to Cai et al. (2023). They establish uncoupled last-iterate convergence along a single trajectory under a uniform hitting time assumption: under every stationary policy pair, the expected time to reach any state from any other state is at most some finite L. Their rate is ˜O(t−1/(9+ ν)) for any fixed ν > 0. This leaves the following question: 

Can uncoupled learners achieve a ˜O(t−1/4) last-iterate rate in unknown Markov games along a single trajectory, under the same hitting time assumption?

Technical ideas. ARTD combines log-barrier policy updates with temporal difference (TD) value estimation and an adaptive regularization schedule. A key difficulty in Cai et al. (2023) is tracking equilibria of regularized local games whose payoffs change with the value estimates, even between consecutive visits to the same state. Slowing value updates stabilizes policy learning but also delays value estimation. Our algorithm separates these two roles. A fast moving average estimates TD targets, while clipped corrections update the values used in policy learning only after every state has supplied a transition. This limits both the size and frequency of target changes while allowing faster statistical estimation. Our analysis further replaces the moving equilibrium comparator with a reference policy that updates using exact payoffs, so its movement is controlled directly by the update size. Log-barrier contraction then controls the played policies’ tracking error together with the TD estimation error. To make this local control imply convergence in the Markov game, we adapt the regularization level to the observed TD discrepancy: when value estimates lag behind, regularization decreases more slowly. This lets us bound both the local duality gaps and the Bellman residual throughout learning. Uncoupled learning with trajectory feedback. Without loss of generality, we fix an initial state s0 ∈ S . The players learn along a single trajectory starting from s0. At round t = 0 , 1, . . . ,they use policies xt ∈ Πx and yt ∈ Πy and sample at ∼ xt(· | st) and bt ∼ yt(· | st) using independent 3private randomness. Cai et al. (2023) maintain a policy and a value estimate at each state. Their policies learn regularized local games whose payoffs combine immediate rewards with estimated future values, while temporal difference (TD) updates refine the value estimates using observed rewards and transitions. These estimates both average noisy samples and define the local games, coupling statistical estimation with the need to keep the policy learning targets slowly varying. We separate these roles: a fast moving average estimates the TD target, while bounded value updates control the motion of the local games. Within these slowly changing games, we use log barrier regularization to stabilize policy updates from bandit feedback. Our Adaptive Regularized TD Learning (ARTD) algorithm uses the TD discrepancy to determine how quickly to decrease the regularization level, keeping policy learning and value estimation at a common accuracy scale.

# 5 Related Work 

Last-iterate convergence. With full gradient feedback, optimistic and extragradient methods have been extensively studied for last-iterate convergence in matrix games, saddle point optimization, 9and monotone variational inequalities (Daskalakis and Panageas, 2019; Mertikopoulos et al., 2019; Golowich et al., 2020; Wei et al., 2021a; Cai et al., 2022). Under bandit feedback, recent work develops uncoupled learning dynamics for zero-sum games, monotone games, and bilinear saddle point problems (Cai et al., 2023; Dong et al., 2025; Maiti et al., 2026). A complementary approach converts average-iterate guarantees into last-iterate guarantees through a black-box reduction (Cai et al., 2025). For uncoupled learning in matrix games with bandit feedback, Fiegel et al. (2025) establish a t−1/4 lower bound for L2 last-iterate convergence, and Fiegel et al. (2026) obtain a matching ˜O(t−1/4) high-probability guarantee using log-barrier regularization. Our algorithm also uses the log barrier, but must jointly control policy learning and changing continuation values from a single trajectory. When opponent actions are additionally observed, faster last-iterate rates are possible (Hait et al., 2026; Zhang, 2026). Our two players learn independently, without observing each other’s actions or communicating with each other. 

Markov games. Markov games provide a standard framework for sequential multi-agent decision making (Shapley, 1953; Littman, 1994). A substantial literature studies sample-efficient equilibrium learning through online exploration and self-play (Bai and Jin, 2020; Bai et al., 2020; Liu et al., 2021; Jin et al., 2024), as well as learning with a generative model (Zhang et al., 2020). Offline equilibrium learning instead uses a fixed dataset, with guarantees governed by coverage and function approximation (Cui and Du, 2022; Zhong et al., 2022; Zhang et al., 2023). For policy optimization with full information, entropy regularization and optimistic updates yield last-iterate guarantees in zero-sum Markov games (Cen et al., 2021, 2023). Another line studies decentralized learning dynamics, including asymptotic convergence and rationality, optimistic policy updates, and finite-sample analyses of learning based on best responses (Sayin et al., 2021; Wei et al., 2021b; Chen et al., 2024). Most directly related to our setting, Cai et al. (2023) establish last-iterate convergence for uncoupled learning along a single trajectory with bandit feedback. Under the same feedback model and uniform hitting assumption, our algorithm ARTD improves their ˜O(t−1/(9+ ν)) rate, for any fixed ν > 0, to ˜O(t−1/4), while requiring no knowledge of the hitting time bound, the time horizon, or the confidence level. 

# 6 Conclusion 

We study uncoupled last-iterate learning in unknown two-player zero-sum discounted Markov games with bandit feedback. Under the uniform hitting time assumption, our algorithm ARTD achieves a 

> ˜

O(t−1/4) duality gap bound for the current policies, with high probability simultaneously over all rounds and starting states. This improves the ˜O(t−1/(9+ ν)) rate of Cai et al. (2023), for any fixed 

ν > 0, under the same feedback model and hitting time assumption. ARTD learns along a single trajectory and requires no knowledge of the hitting time bound, the time horizon, or the confidence level. Our design separates fast temporal difference averaging from bounded value updates, allowing statistical estimation to proceed while limiting changes in the targets used for policy learning. We control the resulting policy error by tracking a reference policy and adapt regularization to the progress of value estimation. By reconciling fast value estimation with stable policy learning, ARTD achieves the improved last-iterate rate within the same uncoupled learning model.

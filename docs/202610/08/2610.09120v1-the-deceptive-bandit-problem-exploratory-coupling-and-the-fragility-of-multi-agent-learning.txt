# The Deceptive Bandit Problem: Exploratory Coupling and the Fragility of Multi-Agent Learning 

Michael Tang, Mahmoud Abdelgalil, Jorge I. Poveda 

Abstract — Randomized exploration is central to bandit learn-ing, multi-agent reinforcement learning, and zeroth-order policy search, yet its independence and privacy are usually only treated as technical assumptions. We show that these properties are critical for security purposes and demonstrate how an adversarial agent can exploit privileged information on another agent’s exploration. We analyze a deceiver-victim pair in the minimal two-player strongly monotone setting, where a decep-tive player obtains leaked signals that are merely correlated 

with the victim’s exploration. We show that, by coupling their own exploratory action with this information, the deceptive player injects an externality that steers the learning dynamics to a new steady state, called the deceptive Nash equilibrium (DNE) . We prove that the deceptive bandit learning (DBL) dynamics converge to an arbitrarily small neighborhood of the DNE while retaining optimal convergence rates. Interestingly, our analysis attains these optimal rates while relaxing second-order smoothness conditions from standard bandit optimization literature. We characterize conditions under which deception strictly shifts the steady state and its effect on the deceiver’s cost, illustrating the results in a resource-allocation game. 

I. I NTRODUCTION 

Multi-agent learning has attracted significant attention in recent years with applications spanning many domains. In many of these applications, agents do not have access to gra-dient information and must instead rely solely on bandit feed-back [1], i.e., measurements of their cost/payoff functions, to update their policies. This has motivated a rich body of literature on multi-agent bandit learning , where agents simul-taneously explore their action spaces and exploit observed costs/payoffs to find optimal policies. Example applications include resource allocation [2], distributed and networked systems [3]–[5], robotics [6], and financial decision-making [7], [8]. More broadly, randomized exploration is a basic ingredient of many learning methods, including stochastic approximation [9], multi-agent reinforcement learning [10], and zeroth-order policy search [11]. If agents learn through bandit feedback, the convergence to the Nash equilibrium (NE) [12]–[14] relies on the assumption that the exploratory policies used by different agents are independent and private .However, in an open, networked, or competitive environment, such an assumption is fragile; an adversarial agent can in-tercept or infer privileged information about the exploratory actions of other agents [15]–[17].

Contributions: We expose a security vulnerability in exploration-driven multi-agent learning: information corre-lated with an agent’s exploratory behavior can be amplified into a persistent bias that changes the long-term outcome of learning. To isolate the core mechanism, we formalize it in two-player strongly monotone games, where a deceptive player modifies only its own exploration using a noisy signal correlated with the oblivious player’s current dither; exten-sions to N -player settings are also discussed. This unilateral coupling induces an externality in the oblivious player’s gradient estimate, scaled by the dither-signal correlation term. We prove that the resulting deceptive bandit learning 

(DBL) dynamics converge to a small neighborhood of a new steady state, a deceptive Nash equilibrium (DNE), with non-asymptotic mean-square guarantees. Our analysis attains the optimal convergence rates from [18] while relaxing their second-order smoothness conditions on the players’ costs; to our knowledge, this relaxation is also novel in the deception-free setting. We characterize when deception strictly shifts the equilibrium and provide geometric conditions under which this shift improves the deceiver’s steady-state cost. Conversely, we also outline some defense strategies against deception that eliminate the induced bias. We then illustrate our main results on a resource-allocation game. 

Related Literature: Other works that study the vulner-abilities of multi-agent learning algorithms include utility measurement poisoning [19], [20], adversarial policies [21], and continuous-time equilibrium seeking [22], [23]. Un-like [19]–[21], the deceiver in our setting never tampers with the victim’s cost measurements, but manipulates learn-ing solely through its own feasible exploratory action. In [22], the deterministic sinusoidal probe structure means that knowledge of the victim’s exploration frequency suffices to reproduce its exploration signal; [23] requires exact real-time measurements of an OU-driven probe. Both study unconstrained continuous-time dynamics and establish local practical asymptotic guarantees under multiple vanishing pa-rameter limits. In contrast, our setting does not require exact reconstruction or real-time measurements of the exploration signal; mere correlation suffices, as our deceiver receives only a noisy, possibly intermittent, randomly transformed, history-adaptive signal correlated with the current dither. We show that the conditional cross-covariance between leakage and exploration exactly governs the induced bias in the vic-tim’s gradient estimator. For constrained, discrete-time bandit play, this yields, to our knowledge, the first non-asymptotic analysis of adversarial exploration coupling in multi-agent learning, with mean-square convergence guarantees. arXiv:2610.09120v1 [cs.LG] 6 Oct 2026 II. Under Assumptions 1-2 and independent exploration, these dynamics are known to efficiently learn the unique NE. IV. B ANDIT FEEDBACK WITH DECEPTION 

The convergence guarantees of exploration-based algo-rithms, such as (5)-(6), rely on the premise that agents conduct their exploration independently and without inter-ference. However, in competitive, adversarial, or asymmetric environments, these independence and privacy assumptions can break down [15]–[17]. In games, the signals available to the players form an information structure separate from the actions and costs [28]. Models of espionage or information leakage study settings where one player receives noisy or partial information about an opponent’s selected strategy or realized randomization [29]–[31]. These works motivate the need to examine analogous asymmetries in bandit learning, where the leaked randomization is now the mechanism by which each player learns. Specifically, we consider a setting where player 2 inter-cepts or infers privileged information regarding player 1’s search policy. If player 2 integrates this information into only their own actions, they can manipulate the gradient estimate that player 1 uses to learn. Here, we call player 2 the 

deceptive player, while player 1 is the oblivious player. This section formalizes the structure of the leaked information, the DBL dynamics, and their long-term effects on learning.

B. Characterization of the DNE and Immunity Conditions 

We established convergence to a neighborhood of the DNE, which is characterized by the variational inequality (14). It is evident from (14) that the steady state conditions are altered under deception, but a fundamental question re-mains: when is the DNE actually different from the nominal NE? Equation (18) bounds the magnitude of this shift, but we now ask when it is nonzero. Furthermore, in what situations does deception actually improve the cost of the deceiver? To isolate the effect of the deception from the com-plexities of active constraints, our vulnerability characteri-zation focuses on interior nominal NE, which is a practical operating condition in many real-world settings [32]–[34]. Specifically, we find that interior NE are locally steerable via deception whenever the externality Γ⊤∇2J1(·) is nonzero at the nominal NE. Conversely, the equilibrium is invariant whenever Γ⊤∇2J1(x∗) = 0 , even if x∗ is not necessarily an interior NE. Thus, the zero-correlation condition Γ = 0 from Remark 1 provides a sufficient universal defense against this particular deception mechanism. VIII. C ONCLUSION 

We introduced a novel framework for deceptive bandit learning in strongly monotone games, demonstrating that noisy information correlated with an opponent’s exploration can induce an externality that steers the joint action profile toward a deceptive Nash equilibrium. We then proved that the DBL dynamics converge to a O(δ) RMS neighborhood of the DNE, establishing optimal rates under weaker regularity conditions than [18] and revealing a novel relaxation that also applies in the deception-free setting. Furthermore, we estab-lished geometric conditions under which the deception mech-anism is guaranteed to shift the steady state and improve the deceiver’s long-term cost/payoff. Our results were illustrated in a two-player resource allocation setting.
